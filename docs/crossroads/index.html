The arithmetic and harmonic fills are mirror images. Inversion swaps the two means, and every hexany or dekany has its inversion partner in the same family, so the AM fill of a subset is the HM fill of its partner turned upside down, with proportional and subcontrary counts swapped. It held on 126 of 126 pairs. The meta-slendro recurrence H[n−2] + H[n−3] is iterated arithmetic division too, because a power-of-two recurrence makes each new term the arithmetic mean of two earlier ones. On the 1·3·5·7 hexany it agrees with Ptolemy's division on three of four tones.

Erv's reasoning, derived

The 14-tone dekanies of Erv Wilson and Kraig Grady

Result as recorded
Wilson and Grady's 14-tone constant structure joins the 2)5 and 3)5 dekanies of 1·3·5·7·9 (Scala archive, "Wilson-Grady 1-3-5-7-9 doubledekany"). Kraig told Marcus that the 1·3·7·9·15 dekany is not a constant structure by itself and needs 4 added tones.
Reasoning derived
Fill one level of Pascal's triangle with tones from the neighbouring level of the same genus. With 1 among the seeds the two dekanies share six tones, so their union has 14, and the 4 tones each dekany is missing come from the other one.
Verified
The machine search agrees. Each 1·3·5·7·9 dekany needs exactly 4 genus tones to become a CS, no 3-tone fill exists, and one of the two minimal fills is the other dekany. Kraig's 1·3·7·9·15 dekany needs 4 tones too, but no fill of 4 or fewer comes from its genus. Both facts are pinned in the tests.
Still a guess
Which 4 tones Kraig actually added to 1·3·7·9·15. I have not found them in the scans, and the machine's fills bring in 13, 17 or 19.
Boundary
The neighbouring-level method works only while the genus has tones in the right places. Where it does not (Kraig's dekany, the canonical dekanies fixing 3 or 7), the fill has to come from outside the genus: epimoric division, a recurrence or a MOS. Erv did this by hand, one structure at a time. A machine can try every method on every subset, which is what the workbench does.

Archytas's enharmonic

Result as recorded
Archytas (about 400 BCE) divided the fourth as 5/4 · 36/35 · 28/27.
Reasoning derived
Wilson's two-step mediant cascade. The mediant of 1/1 and 4/3 is 5/4, and the mediant of 5/4 and 4/3 is 9/7. Neighbours on the Stern–Brocot tree always differ by an epimoric ratio (n+1)/n.
Verified
Exact rationals, pinned in the tests. Of eight classical genera, only Archytas's enharmonic is a mediant cascade. Ptolemy's come from arithmetic division instead.
Still a guess
Whether Erv derived his mediant cascades from Archytas or found the same tree on his own through the scale tree.
Boundary
Archytas's theorem: an epimore has no rational half, so every just split of an epimoric gap is lopsided. This is also why geometric triads are rare in a CPS.
