Lattice MOS-Fit Playground

Start from a just lattice. Find the MOS scales that hold every tone of the lattice within a tolerance. An MOS is a chain of one generator with two step sizes, and equal temperaments are the MOS with one step size. The fit has three controls: the generator, the level (how many notes) and the murchana (which chain position is the 1/1). Within 2 cents counts as at least as good as 12-ET.

STATION 3

Fit an MOS to the lattice

Two searches. The first table takes the station 2 generator and, for each level, finds the murchana that holds the lattice best. The second table is the free search: for each N it finds the best generator of all, with its murchana. Select a row to see the scale against the lattice, then rotate its murchana.

What the fit searches, in Wilson's terms. A scale here is a chain: N consecutive generators, reduced to one octave. The octave is the period, and it does not change. The generator g runs over 0 to 600 cents in the free search, and its complement covers the rest. The note count N is the level. The "only MOS" switch keeps the N values that are levels on the scale tree. The window start is the murchana. The chain contains the lattice's 1/1 at one of its N degrees. The search tries all N positions, which is the same as trying every mode of the chain. For each N the free search is exact. For a tolerance ε, each tone and each degree k allow g to lie in an interval of half-width ε/|k|. A sweep over the interval endpoints finds the first g where some window of N degrees holds every tone. A bisection on ε then finds the smallest ε that works. A final refinement moves g to minimize the largest tone error.

What the fit does not do. It does not change the period. It does not place the lattice's 1/1 between two degrees. It fits tones and not triads, and it reports the triads that survive so you can see the difference. It uses one generator, so it is rank 2 with the octave. A lattice that needs two generators, such as a 7-limit CPS at a small N, gets a large error here.

2 cents is below the 12-ET error of 3/2 (1.96 cents) and far below its error of 5/4 (13.7 cents).

Press Run. Large lattices at large N take a few seconds.

BACKGROUND

What the mathematics says

A chain is N consecutive multiples of one generator, reduced to one octave. When the chain has exactly two step sizes it is an MOS, and N is a level of that generator. The levels are the denominators on the Stern–Brocot path toward g. Erv called this path the scale tree and drew it as a zig-zag on each of his generator pages.

Erv Wilson, "Scale Tree" and the Meru pages, 1993–1997.

An equal temperament is the MOS with one step size. N-ET is the N-note chain of the generator 1200/N cents. Treating ET as a separate category is a preference of the musician, not a fact of the mathematics. This page shows ET rows separately because some artists prefer ET.

Marcus Hobbs, 2026-10-11.

Murchana is the mode of the chain. The chain holds the lattice's 1/1 at one of its N generator positions. Moving that position by one drops a tone from one end and adds one at the other end, which rotates the step pattern. For a fifth generator at N = 7 the seven positions give the seven diatonic modes, Lydian through Locrian.

Checked on this page: meta-meantone at N = 7 gives LLLsLLs, LLsLLLs, LLsLLsL, LsLLLsL, LsLLsLL, sLLLsLL, sLLsLLL.

Inverting the generator inverts the lattice. The chain of 1 − g is the chain of g reflected about 1/1. So a lattice fits the complement generator exactly when its reflection fits g. For a CPS built from factors F, the reflection of CPS(n, k) is CPS(n, n − k) transposed by the product of F. So the hexany, the eikosany and CPS(8, 4) are their own reflections, and the tetrad, the dekany and the pentadekany swap with their partners. One fit of a dekany solves both dekanies.

Verified 2026-10-11 with exact rationals. The reflected hexany fits the same chains at the mirrored murchana.

The free search gives a staircase of smallest errors. For the hexany it is 18.85 cents at N = 11 and 13.73 at N = 12 to 14. It is 4.82 at N = 15, 4.77 at 16 to 17 and 3.27 at 18. It is 2.57 at N = 19 to 21, 1.37 at 22 to 30 and 0.74 at 31. Every optimum from N = 11 up is a val: an integer mapping of the primes 3, 5, 7 to generator counts. The N = 15 fit at 502.86 cents is a septimal meantone and is Erv's 0.418935 generator to 0.14 cents. The N = 22 fit at 193.79 cents has kernel 2401/2400 and 3136/3125. No equal temperament below 73 notes holds the hexany within 2 cents.

CPSFIT-001, experiments/lattice, 2026-10-10.

Fitting tones is not fitting triads. A prime mistuned in all three tones of a triad cancels in the mean condition. So a chain with large tone errors can keep triads. A chain with small tone errors can lose them. At N = 22 the tone fit keeps 10 of the hexany's 12 triads at 2 cents. Minimizing the worst triad instead moves the generator to 193.91 cents, keeps all 12, and lets one tone out to 2.22 cents. The worst triad is still not the count: at N = 18 to 21 the chain with the smaller worst triad keeps fewer triads. This page reports tones and triads side by side.

CPSFIT-001 and CPSFIT-002, experiments/lattice, 2026-10-10.

The hexany needs the primes 3, 5 and 7, which is rank 4 with the octave. One generator and the octave is rank 2. So the hexany fits a rank-2 chain only with error, or only at a large N where the chain's commas vanish. A fit with two generators is rank 3 and is the natural next search.

OPTIONS

Next lines of research

  1. Count the triads instead of the worst one (CPSFIT-003). For each N, find the generator and murchana that keep the most lattice triads within the tolerance. CPSFIT-002 showed that the worst triad and the count disagree at N = 18 to 21. The frozen triad scorer referees.
  2. Rank 3. Search two generators and a note count, and keep the lattice within tolerance. The hexany, the dekany and the eikosany need this below N = 22. This is the D'Alessandro method applied to a general seed.
  3. Fit the subsets. Fit each hexany inside the eikosany separately, then ask which chain holds the most of the fifteen at once. The subset doctrine says the music lives in the subsets.
  4. Quotient the ties. When an N-chain of g holds the lattice, the 2N-chain of g/2 holds it too with the same errors. Report one representative per family so the staircase reads cleanly.
  5. Hear the fits. Export the selected chain as Scala text, load it in the plugin, and listen to the lattice tones inside the chain. Gates G-029 and G-031 wait on this for the hexany at N = 15 and N = 22.
  6. Murchana as a performance control. A chain that holds the lattice at one murchana holds a transposed copy of it at the others. Map the murchana to a controller and play the lattice through its modes.
